10-K research assistant

Johns Hopkins team project, six people

  • LangChain
  • Gemini
  • Ollama
  • Streamlit
  • Python
1The question
Annual reports run to hundreds of pages. Can an analyst ask plain-English questions of Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft's 10-K filings and get answers grounded in the documents themselves?
2What I did
  • Worked in a six-person team to build a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system in LangChain. The filings are split into passages, the passages most relevant to a question are retrieved, and the language model answers from that retrieved text instead of from memory.
  • Grounding answers in the source is the point: a model answering from memory can confidently invent a revenue figure, while one answering from the filing can be checked against it.
  • The stack combined Gemini, Ollama for running models locally, and a Streamlit interface so anyone could ask questions in a browser without touching code.
  • During final integration I compared the teammates' notebooks against the production app and found three fixes that had never made it into app.py. I merged them before submission.
3What came of it
  • A working research assistant that answers questions about three of the world's largest companies using their own disclosures.
  • A perfect score of 30 out of 30.
  • The lesson I carry forward: in team projects, quality is won or lost at integration. Checking that everyone's work actually made it into the final product is unglamorous, and it's exactly what protected the grade.

Bruker (BRKR) valuation

University of Miami Student Managed Investment Fund

  • Bloomberg Terminal
  • Comparable companies
  • Excel
1The question
Is Bruker, a maker of scientific instruments, priced attractively relative to companies like it, and does it deserve a place in the fund?
2What I did
  • Built a peer set of comparable companies and pulled their financials and trading multiples from the Bloomberg Terminal.
  • Benchmarked Bruker's valuation against that peer set to see whether the market was pricing it at a premium or a discount, and what might justify the gap.
  • Weighed the upside against the risks and concluded the stock didn't justify adding to or cutting the position, so I recommended a hold. I wrote it up as a formal recommendation and defended it before the fund's investment committee, where every call had to survive questioning.
3What came of it
  • The committee agreed and the fund held the position. A hold is a real decision too: it means the analysis found no edge worth trading on, and saying so is part of managing money responsibly. The fund grew from about $1.7M to $2.1M during my time as an analyst.
  • Fluency with Bloomberg that I later passed on, coaching a group of fellow students through reading Terminal data and building their own valuations.
  • Practice in the core analyst habit: forming a view, then stress-testing it in front of people whose job is to find the holes.
